I can take the mean of `random_samp` because it is numeric and the mean
of `vec_logical` because R treats `TRUE` as 1 and `FALSE` as 0.
Therefore, the mean of `vec_logical` represents the proportion of
observations in `random_samp` that are greater than 0. I cannot take the
mean of `vec_char` because character strings cannot be averaged, nor can
I take the mean of `vec_factor` because factors are categorical
variables.

``` r
as.numeric(pull(samp_df, vec_logical))
as.numeric(pull(samp_df, vec_char))
as.numeric(pull(samp_df, vec_factor))
```

Applying `as.numeric()` to the logical variable converts `TRUE` and
`FALSE` to 1 and 0, respectively. Applying `as.numeric()` to the
character variable produces `NA` values because the character strings
cannot be interpreted as numbers. Applying `as.numeric()` to the factor
variable returns the underlying integer codes assigned to its factor
levels rather than the original labels. This helps explain why taking
the mean of the logical variable works, while taking the mean of the
character and factor variables does not directly produce a meaningful
result.
